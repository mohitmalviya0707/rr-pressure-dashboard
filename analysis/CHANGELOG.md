# Change log: v2 → v3

Every output was recomputed from the raw data pack. No flags, rankings or intermediates from v2 were reused.

| # | Problem in v2 | Fix in v3 | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Within innings" thresholds were pooled by innings number, so the share of pressure balls per match-innings ranged from 0% to 100% (281 of 1,652 innings had none). | Real per match+innings thresholds (definition A). A per innings-number variant (B) and the absolute definition (C) were also built, and all three compared on split-half reliability. C is primary: it had ρ 0.31 bat / 0.23 bowl, against A at 0.09 / −0.01 and B at 0.10 / 0.09. A and B stay in the consensus. | Makes the definition explicit and testable. The definition was chosen on measured reliability, with the trade-off disclosed: 99.9% of C's pressure balls are in chases. Batting-first vs chasing splits (under A) are reported per pick. |
| 2 | Emerging/Rising Stars A-team T20s (6,973 rows) were pooled into the T20I model tables. | Scope is built from competitions.csv: IPL plus official T20I only. Excluded competitions are dropped before any modelling. IPL and T20I each get their own outcome tables, DP, recalibration and expectations. | Out-of-scope cricket can no longer influence any number. |
| 3 | 24 no-result matches (1,753 balls) were inside the ranking scope. Tied games were labelled with the super-over winner. | No-result/abandoned matches are excluded everywhere. So are DLS matches without a recorded revised target (6 in scope) and super overs (26 balls). Ties count as 0.5. DLS matches with a target are kept. | No-result and target-less states have no valid win probability. A tie is a regulation result. |
| 4 | WP validation was in-sample, and recalibration was pooled across formats. | 5-fold out-of-fold validation grouped by match (AUC/Brier/ECE for chase and first innings, by format). Recalibration is per format × innings. Added a DP vs Monte-Carlo check, a model vs actual check on expected runs, and an empirical check on wicket value. | Removes leakage; validates the Markov model itself. |
| 5 | The size of extras was implicitly assumed, extras_2 was charged to bowlers, and economy was presented as exact. | extras1_runs was audited as a type code. Assumptions A1–A4 are stated and checked on every ball. extras_2 is not charged to bowlers. Economy is labelled as a reconstructed lower bound; runs off the bat per over is used as the verified field. A bat-runs-only sensitivity run gives Spearman 0.993 and 10/10 of the top 10 unchanged. Team totals were checked against official margins (MAE 2.12 runs). | Separates verified from proxy metrics. |
| 6 | EB k bootstrap resampled with replacement, which biases k downward. | Stability is checked by 50% match subsampling without replacement, plus rankings re-run at the 5th and 95th percentile of k. | k is imprecise, but rankings are insensitive to it (Spearman ≥ 0.994). |
| 7 | audit.py reused the engine's flags, and some checks compared nothing. | audit.py is independent (it does not import engine). It recomputes scope, filters, extras, flags, all 11 model runs, consensus, eligibility, k and all 20 picks from raw rows. 64 checks were executed and all passed. | The audit can now actually catch engine errors. |
| 8 | The deck data script exec'd the report script. The dashboard had different defaults from Python. | deck_data.py is standalone. The dashboard defaults to C and uses the same 10 scenarios. lab/crosscheck.py runs the page headless and matches every rank, level and k for all 11 runs. | Rankings are consistent across CSVs, dashboard and deck. |
| 9 | Some claims were unsupported (e.g. "pressure balls are riskier"; nationality and availability details). | League rates under C are nearly identical for pressure and ordinary balls, so that claim was removed. Nationality comes from the data, with 3 public-record fills labelled. Availability states only the IPL 2026 team in the data, plus confirmed trades (MyKhel, 8 Nov 2026). Retention lists are due 17 Nov 2026 and are not yet public. | No invented retention, auction or squad information. |
| 10 | The 11-run consensus could be decided by one setting. Five picks (Klaasen, Rickelton, Hendricks, Ferguson, Santner) fell away under a wider stress test. | Added stress.py (600 settings) and final_select.py (reliability-weighted stability score). The audit independently recomputes 40 sampled grid runs, their split-half weights and the final rule. 15 of 20 picks are unchanged. New picks: Abhishek Sharma, Mitchell Marsh, Fakhar Zaman, Jasprit Bumrah, Abrar Ahmed. | The final lists hold across far more reasonable settings, including recent form (2024+). The rule was adopted after the first results, and that is disclosed. |
